# Herbarium labels: the three specimen paintings

The label is a botanic-garden specimen sheet, because vanilla reached Ceylon through the 19th-century botanic gardens.
Each product gets its own specimen, the part of the plant its story is about:

| | Specimen | Why |
|---|---|---|
| No. 01 With alcohol | Three cured pods with a leafy stem and one flower | The classic extract: the cured pod at full strength |
| No. 02 Alcohol-free | A flowering vine tip: two flowers, buds, leaves | The gentle one |
| No. 03 Syrup | A stem with a bunch of green pods | The sweet one: the fruit |

The painting holds only the plant. The coloured tapes, the stamp, the slip and all the type are added in the artwork, so
they stay sharp and in each product's colour.

Attach:
1. `../assets/nihonga-moodboard.jpg`: the technique (1–3) and our vanilla (4).
2. `board-specimen-slot.jpg`: where the specimen sits on the front.

Make No. 01 first. For No. 02 and No. 03, also attach the No. 01 result and start with the companion line.

## Prompt (No. 01)

> Paint a single vanilla plant specimen. Plant style: exactly like reference 4 on the moodboard (our vanilla painting):
> blue-green leaves with soft yellow-green edges, a cream vanilla orchid with a golden trumpet-shaped lip, matte
> dark-brown cured pods with curled tips. Technique: like references 1 to 3: smooth simplified forms, a fine stippled
> grain, soft edges, almost no surface detail, japanese nihonga on gofun silk paper. Not photorealistic: no wrinkles,
> no leaf veins, no bark texture.
>
> It is a pressed herbarium specimen. The plant is laid completely flat and seen straight from above, as if pressed
> between sheets of paper, its colours slightly faded and gentle. A botanist has arranged it: balanced and centred, the
> stems crossing gracefully, nothing bunched or overlapping awkwardly.
>
> The specimen: three cured vanilla pods lying side by side in a gentle fan, crossed by a short leafy stem with two
> leaves and one open vanilla flower.
>
> Plain, flat, warm cream paper background #F7F0DF. No shadow, no tape, no pins, no labels, no text, no frame. The
> specimen fills about 80% of the width with clear space all round, and nothing touches the edges. Wide landscape 3:2.

## No. 02 and No. 03

Start with:

> A companion to the attached No. 01 specimen: the same scale, plant style, technique, paper and light. Only the
> specimen changes:

then use the No. 01 prompt with the specimen line swapped:

- **No. 02:** "A flowering vanilla vine tip: a slender stem with three alternate leaves, two open vanilla flowers and
  a small cluster of closed buds."
- **No. 03:** "A short vanilla stem with two leaves and a hanging bunch of five long, slim green vanilla pods, fanned
  out flat."

## Checks

- Flat, from above: no perspective and no cast shadow. It has to look pressed.
- It reads as vanilla: a trumpet-lipped flower, long slim pods, fleshy alternate leaves.
- Plain cream behind it, so it drops onto the label paper with no cut-out halo.
- The three come out at the same scale and footprint.
