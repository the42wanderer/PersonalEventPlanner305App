# Tote bag: the open brief

No reference images. Paste the prompt into ChatGPT as it is. It asks GPT to come up with the idea itself, so it is
written like a brief to an illustrator: what the bag is for, what success looks like, and the hard limits of the
print. It leaves the picture open.

## Prompt

> You are a top illustrator and print designer. Design the artwork for a canvas tote bag for **The Vanilla Shop in
> Negombo, Sri Lanka**. It will be screen-printed in **one black ink on natural, unbleached cotton**.
>
> **Before you draw, think it through.** Write three different one-line concepts. Choose the one that would make a
> stranger on the street ask "where did you get that bag?", and say in one sentence why. Then make that one.
>
> **What the bag is for.**
> - It is the shop's walking billboard. Customers carry it round Negombo.
> - Negombo is the airport town, the first and last night of most trips to Sri Lanka, so many people will take it
>   home as their keepsake of the island.
> - It has to work three ways:
>   - **From five metres:** one bold, simple idea that reads at a glance.
>   - **From fifty centimetres:** details that reward a closer look.
>   - **On Instagram:** a flat-lay that people want to post.
>
> **The brand.** The Vanilla Shop sells real Ceylon vanilla (extracts, pods, paste, a café) from vines grown in
> Sri Lankan home gardens.
> - **What makes it special:** every vanilla flower opens for a single morning and has to be pollinated by hand,
>   one by one. Then each green pod is cured for months, in the sun by day and wrapped at night, until it turns
>   dark and fragrant.
> - **Its lines:** "Savor the calm" and "Vanilla was never plain". The second is the attitude to aim for: vanilla
>   is usually a byword for boring, and this bag should prove the opposite.
>
> **Territory to draw from** (use what serves your idea, not all of it):
> - the hand-pollination ritual;
> - the vine and its flower;
> - the long, slim green pods hanging in bunches;
> - the sun-curing;
> - Negombo itself: the lagoon, the outrigger canoes with their big curved sails, fishermen and the fish market,
>   planes coming in low over the beach, the old canal, the sea.
>
> **Draw the vanilla correctly.**
> - **The flower** is a pale orchid: three narrow sepals and two petals spread like a loose star, round a lip
>   rolled into a trumpet with a frilled edge. It is not a daffodil, lily or frangipani.
> - **The pods** are long, pencil-thin and gently curved, hanging in loose bunches. They are not fat or
>   banana-like.
> - **The leaves** are thick, fleshy and oval, placed alternately along a smooth green vine.
>
> **Lettering.**
> - Include "THE VANILLA SHOP" and "NEGOMBO", spelled exactly like that.
> - You may add "SRI LANKA" and one of the two lines above, and nothing else.
> - English only.
> - Make the lettering part of the design, drawn with the same confidence as the picture, not a caption added
>   underneath.
>
> **What good looks like.**
> - The best independent bookshop, coffee roaster and museum totes: one strong idea, wit or surprise,
>   unexpected scale, generous empty canvas, and nothing that isn't needed.
> - It should look drawn by a skilled human hand. Choose one way of drawing and commit to it:
>   - carved linocut;
>   - bold brush and ink;
>   - or a confident pen line.
> - The line has character and small irregularities, and the composition is decisive, not evenly busy.
>
> **Avoid what makes images look generated:**
> - glossy rendering or smooth gradients;
> - detail spread evenly over everything;
> - ornamental borders stuffed with filler;
> - fake "vintage" distress laid over the top;
> - symmetrical clip-art arrangements;
> - a collage of tourist icons.
>
> Also avoid the clichés: no palm-and-sunset postcard, no elephants, no tea pluckers. Use no religious imagery,
> flags or national emblems. If you draw people, draw them with dignity and keep them simple; no caricature.
>
> **Hard limits for the print.**
> - **One colour:** solid black ink, plus the bare canvas as the second colour. No greys, no halftone dots, no
>   gradients, no white ink.
> - **Size:** the design fits a print area of 27 × 34 cm, portrait.
> - **Line weight:** nothing thinner than about 0.5 mm at that size; no gaps that would fill in.
> - **Small lettering:** at least 5 mm tall.
> - **Coverage:** keep the solid black under about 45% of the area, so the bag stays soft to carry.
>
> **Output.** The flat artwork only, seen straight on and centred on a plain unbleached-canvas-coloured
> background, portrait 4:5, with the design filling most of the height. No bag, no hands, no props, no mock-up.

## Follow-ups to use in the same conversation

- **Another idea:** "Make concept 2 now, with the same rules."
- **Push it further:** "Bolder: cut half the detail, make the main shape twice as big, and keep only what you'd
  miss."
- **See it on the bag:** "Show this artwork screen-printed in black on a natural canvas tote bag, photographed
  flat on a table in daylight. Keep the artwork exactly as it is."

## Checking a result before sending it to me

- **The idea:** can you say it in one sentence, and does it make you look twice?
- **Five metres:** shrink it to thumbnail size. Does one clear shape still read?
- **The ink:** black and canvas only, with no greys or soft fades.
- **The plant:** the flowers have trumpet lips and the pods are slim.
- **The lettering:** spelled exactly right. If the words are wrong or wobbly, still send it; I'll reset the type
  in vector.

## What I do with it

1. **Trace:** redraw or trace it to clean 1-bit artwork.
2. **Type:** reset the lettering in real fonts if needed.
3. **Print checks:** check line weights and the black coverage.
4. **Print files:** make the single film and a mock-up on canvas.
