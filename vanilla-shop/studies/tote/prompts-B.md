# Tote bag, direction B: Ceylon Post, the vanilla garden

Attach `tote-B-ai-references.jpg` (numbered 1 to 9) with the prompt.

## Prompt 1: the stamp (art side)

Using the attached reference board, create one large vintage postage stamp in the manner of the 1930s to 1950s
Ceylon pictorial stamps (references 1 and 3). Recess-engraved line art: crisp burin hatching and cross-hatching,
no soft shading, no gradients, no photographic texture. Exactly two flat ink colours on a plain warm cream ground:
the scene in deep forest green (#143A1D) and the frame in terracotta red (#B5452B). Portrait format, 4:5.

The frame: a classic stamp frame with a toothed, perforated outer edge, a fine inner border with a small repeating
ornamental band like reference 1, a plain banner across the top, a small tablet in the bottom-left corner, and a
plain strip along the bottom. Leave the banner, tablet and strip completely empty. There must be no letters,
numbers or words anywhere in the image, and no portrait of a monarch.

The scene inside the frame: a Sri Lankan village vanilla garden in the early 1900s, in morning light. On the left,
set back, an old village house with smooth hand-plastered mud walls and a deep, heavy thatched roof of dried palm
fronds, its low eaves resting on rough wooden posts, an open doorway, and a swept earth yard in front (references 4
and 5). From the doorway a husband and wife walk out into the garden, unhurried, in the village dress of that time
(reference 5). He wears a plain sarong wrapped at the waist, bare-chested with a cloth over one shoulder, his hair
tied back, and carries a small woven basket. She wears an ankle-length wrapped cloth and a fitted white
short-sleeved jacket, her hair in a low bun, barefoot, and gently lifts a vanilla flower on the vine with one hand.
Across the middle and right of the scene stands a row of slim support trees, lopped at about head height so each
sprouts a crown of small feathery leaves (reference 7). A vanilla vine climbs each trunk with thick, fleshy oval
leaves (reference 8), loops over and hangs back down in long swags, with bunches of long green pods hanging from
it (reference 9) and a few pale orchid flowers. Coconut palms and soft hills behind; the sky mostly open, drawn in
fine horizontal engraved lines.

Composition: from across a street, the scene should read as three clear shapes: the dark mass of the thatched roof
on the left, the two figures in the centre-left foreground, and the rhythm of upright trunks and looping vines on
the right, with the nearest trunk running tall up the right side of the frame and its pods hanging into the
foreground. The figures should be large enough to read, about a third of the scene's height. Keep the lower
foreground calm, so the empty strip at the bottom sits on open ground.

Line quality for screen printing on canvas: bold, open engraving with no hairline detail. Every line and every gap
must be thick enough to survive printing at 30 cm wide; let areas fill in solid where fine engraving would close
up. Flat, high-contrast and vector-like: the image will be traced and printed as two spot colours.

## Prompt 2 (optional): preview on the bag

Show the attached stamp artwork screen-printed large on the front of a natural, unbleached cotton tote bag
(15 × 16 in), lying flat on a wooden table in soft daylight. The ink sits slightly into the canvas weave, with a
little texture from the canvas. Keep the artwork exactly as given, without redrawing it.

## Set by us afterwards, in vector (not by the AI)

- The country's name in the top banner: CEYLON, ලංකා, இலங்கை (as on the 1950s stamps).
- The caption strip: VANILLA GARDEN (the old stamps name their scene, as in "PLUCKING TEA").
- The value in the tablet.
- The postmark: a round cancel over the top-right corner, "THE VANILLA SHOP · NEGOMBO" round the ring, a date in
  the middle (the branch's opening day?), and the logo. A real postmark names its town, so NEGOMBO lives here.
- Slogan side, the back of a postcard: POST CARD in three languages, a centre rule, address lines, a handwritten
  line, and the logo in the stamp box. Candidate lines:
  1. "Wish you were here. Brought you vanilla instead."
  2. "Sending you a little Ceylon."
  3. "Vanilla was never plain." (the line from the tea boxes)
- The Sinhala and Tamil text needs a native speaker's check before print.
