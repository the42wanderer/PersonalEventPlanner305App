# Tote stamp: the redraw

Attach `redraw-references.jpg` (numbered 1 to 12).

## Why the old stamp failed

The last brief said "keep the scene from reference 1", and reference 1 was itself an AI image. Its mistakes were
copied forward:
- daffodil and frangipani flowers instead of vanilla;
- fat, banana-like pods;
- a flower as big as the woman;
- a ladder leaning on nothing;
- a six-fingered hand;
- yellow skin in patches.

This brief uses **no earlier artwork**. The plant comes from photos of real vanilla, including our own pods
(reference 8).

The scene also changes to what really happens in a Sri Lankan vanilla garden:
- The vine is trained on lopped support trees and looped back down, so the flowers hang at chest height. Nobody
  needs a ladder.
- Each flower is pollinated by hand with a thin stick.

## Prompt

Using the attached reference board, draw a vintage Ceylon pictorial postage stamp in the manner of references 2 and
3, with the layout of reference 1. Portrait 4:5.

**The scene.** A small vanilla garden in the hills of central Sri Lanka, in the early morning.
- **The woman.** In the centre-left foreground, a village woman stands on the ground, pollinating a vanilla
  flower. She wears an ankle-length wrapped cloth, a fitted white short-sleeved jacket and her hair in a low bun
  (reference 12).
  - The flower hangs at her chest height on a looped vine (reference 7).
  - With one hand she steadies the small flower. With the other she holds a thin stick to it (reference 6).
  - She is seen in three-quarter view, looking at the flower.
- **The man.** Just behind her, a man in a plain sarong, bare-chested with a cloth over one shoulder, holds a
  woven basket against his hip with both arms around it.
  - In the basket lies a bunch of long, slim green vanilla pods.
- **The figures:**
  - large, together about half the height of the picture, like the tea plucker in reference 2;
  - drawn with calm, correct anatomy;
  - hands small and simple, with fingers together. Where possible a hand holds something or is partly hidden by
    the basket, the cloth or a leaf.
- **The vine.** Behind them and down the right, a row of slim support trees is lopped at about head height, each
  with a crown of small feathery leaves (reference 10). A vanilla vine climbs each trunk, loops over and hangs back
  down in swags (reference 9).
  - **Leaves:** thick, fleshy and oval with pointed tips, placed alternately along a smooth green stem, with a few
    thin aerial roots at the joints.
- **The flowers.** These are the vanilla flowers of references 4 and 5, and must be drawn exactly like them:
  - small, about the size of the woman's hand, in little clusters at the leaf joints, with green buds beside them;
  - each has three narrow pointed sepals and two petals spread like a loose star, plus a **lip rolled into a tube
    or trumpet** with a frilled edge, wrapped round a central column;
  - seen from the side, the lip is a trumpet, as in reference 4.
- **The pods** hang in loose bunches of eight to twelve, exactly like reference 8:
  - long, very slim, round in section, pencil-thick;
  - straight or gently curved;
  - each on its own short stalk from the bunch's top.
- **Behind.** On the left, set well back and small, a village house with smooth mud walls and a deep cadjan thatch
  (reference 11). Low rounded hills, a few areca and coconut palms, and an open sky.

**The vine breaks out of the stamp.** The nearest support tree runs up the right side of the picture. Its vine
grows out over the right frame bar, across the top-right corner and past the edge, and its pod bunches hang down
over the bottom-right of the frame.
- It is in the foreground, so it is drawn larger than the vines behind.
- Its flowers stay true to scale: each flower is smaller than one of its leaves.

**Drawing.** Bold recess-engraved line, like reference 2.
- **One line language everywhere:**
  - the same engraved hatching on the figures, the vine, the house and the sky;
  - no thick outline or halo round any shape;
  - nothing that looks cut out and pasted on.
- **Tone** comes from even, deliberate parallel hatching and cross-hatching, not from scratches or specks.
- **Line weight for screen printing:** every line and every gap is at least 0.5 mm wide when the stamp is printed
  27 cm wide. Where fine engraving would close up, let the area fill in solid.

**Exactly three flat inks on a warm cream paper ground**, like reference 3:
- **Deep forest green (#1E3B24):** all line work and hatching.
- **Vermilion (#D9472B):** only the frame.
- **Marigold (#E8B23A):** only the sky, a light wash on the flowers (they are pale green-yellow) and the mud wall.
- **Cream paper:** skin, clothes, the basket, the flower highlights and the paper margin.
  - Skin is cream paper shaded with green hatching, like the clothes, never yellow.
  - The pods are green hatching over cream, with no yellow tips.

No gradients, no halftone dots, no other colours.

**Frame and edge (reference 1):**
- an even row of large round perforation teeth;
- a clear cream margin;
- a wide plain vermilion frame with a deep empty band across the top;
- a narrow empty cream strip down the picture's left edge;
- a bottom band with an empty square value tablet on the left and an empty cream caption box beside it.

There are no letters, numbers or symbols anywhere: every band, tablet and box stays plain, because the lettering is
added afterwards.

**Avoid:**
- daffodils, frangipani, lilies or any other flower;
- flowers larger than a hand;
- banana-like or chilli-like pods;
- ladders;
- extra fingers or hands;
- yellow skin;
- outlined sticker shapes;
- a sun disc with rays;
- text.

## Checking each result before picking

Generate several and reject any that fail one of these. Zoom in to check:

1. **Hands.** Count the fingers on every visible hand: four fingers and a thumb, or fingers together. No blobs.
2. **Flowers.** Every flower has a trumpet-shaped lip (references 4 and 5). Reject a cup in the middle (daffodil),
   five round petals (frangipani) or a lily shape.
3. **Scale.** No flower is bigger than a hand.
4. **Pods.** Slim and pencil-thick in loose bunches (reference 8), not fat clusters.
5. **People.** Both stand on the ground, and nothing floats. The basket rests on his hip.
6. **Line.** The vine is drawn in the same line as everything else, with no thick outline.
7. **Colour.** Skin and pods carry no yellow; yellow appears only on the sky, the flowers and the wall.
8. **Lettering.** None anywhere.

A result can still be used if only small details fail (one hand, a stray flower): I'll redraw those in vector
during separation.

## Set by us afterwards, in vector

- **Frame and perforations:** rebuilt as clean vector shapes, with even teeth and square corners, replacing the
  generated edge.
- **Top band:** CEYLON, with ලංකා and இலங்கை beside it.
- **Left strip:** VANILLA PLANIFOLIA.
- **Tablet:** 25c.
- **Caption box:** THE VANILLA SHOP, with NEGOMBO beneath.
- **The postmark is dropped,** so the name appears once on the front. The logo is on the back.
- **Separation:** three films (green, vermilion, marigold), with specks below 0.5 mm removed and the marigold kept
  inside the green lines.
- **Before films:** a native speaker checks the Sinhala and Tamil.
